THE IDEA

How can organizations systematically examine whether their AI systems contribute to sustainability and the public interest?

This independent concept explores an AI-assisted self-assessment tool that could help organizations collect and interpret evidence, reflect on benefits and harms, and flag questions for human or independent review. Its workflow could draw on established frameworks, including HIIG’s Impact AI Method, without being limited to one methodology. AI supports the work; people remain responsible for validating evidence and making judgments.

An illustrative self-assessment journey

An illustrative organizational user journey, not a replacement for any framework’s formal assessment process. AI could assist with organizing and reviewing information at each stage; users validate its suggestions and retain control of the conclusions.

Why independent perspectives still matter

An organization assessing its own system may overlook harms or overstate benefits. AI-generated suggestions can also be incomplete or misleading. This proposed tool is a preparatory and reflective aid, not a certification or substitute for independent assessment. Human reviewers must check evidence, consider affected stakeholders, and make normative judgments. Rigorous frameworks such as HIIG’s Impact AI Method additionally rely on interviews, qualitative analysis, and external involvement.

Towards an Integrated AI Impact Assessment Framework

The five-step self-assessment above illustrates one component of a broader research agenda. I am interested in developing an AI-assisted platform across three complementary pillars, with human oversight and independent perspectives throughout.

POTENTIAL RESEARCH PLATFORM

AI-assisted impact assessment

Framework-informed · Evidence-oriented · Human-in-the-loop

01 · STRUCTURED ASSESSMENT Evidence & stakeholder perspectives Framework-guided review of documentation, interviews, governance, and potential harms (e.g., HIIG’s Impact AI Method). What do we know and how credible is the evidence?
02 · QUANTITATIVE Data & causal evaluation Operational and observational data, descriptive statistics, experiments, and causal designs when appropriate. Did deploying the AI system cause a change in outcomes?
03 · INSTITUTIONAL Use & accountability Human review, institutional adoption, governance, and decisions informed by findings. Does the assessment improve decisions and accountability?
